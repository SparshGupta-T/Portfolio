MyCouncillor

Reporting neighbourhood issues and managing ward fixes

MyCouncillor is an app and dashboard that connects local residents directly with their ward councillors. I designed the entire experience—making it effortless for citizens to report problems with photos and GPS, and giving councillors a clean dashboard to assign tasks to repair crews and track fixes to completion.

Core Product Pillars

Four key pillars designed to make reporting effortless for citizens and triage actionable for ward authorities.

1 Photo & GPS Issue Reports

Automatic GPS location detection, manual pin adjustment on the map, clear problem categories (potholes, streetlights, garbage, water), and photo uploads.

2 Live Status Updates

A simple 6-step timeline for every report: Reported → Under Review → Assigned to Crew → Work in Progress → Fixed (with photo) → Citizen Confirmed.

3 Automatic Ward Routing

Directs reports automatically to the right local department and field team based on the incident's GPS coordinates.

4 Councillor Overview Dashboard

A clear overview for councillors and staff showing pending tickets, heatmaps of frequent complaints, and resolution times.

Role & What I Built

Led the end-to-end design, delivering clean Figma components and detailed flow specifications for the engineering team.

  • Designed the resident mobile app from the ground up: simple login, camera uploads, and automatic location detection.
  • Built the councillor tablet and desktop portal for reviewing complaints, dispatching workers, and sending status updates.
  • Added a photo verification rule: repair crews must upload a photo of the completed fix before a ticket can be closed.
  • Designed community features like councillor office hours, public neighbourhood notices, and ongoing local development updates.
  • Created an accessible design system with high-contrast text and clear icons so people of all age groups can use the app comfortably.
How Issues Move From Report to Resolution

Civic tools often fail when reports get lost in bureaucracy. Having clear, visible steps keeps everyone accountable.

  • Created a clear 6-step progress tracker for every ticket: Reported → Under Review → Assigned to Crew → Work in Progress → Fixed (with photo) → Citizen Confirmed.
  • Organized common complaints into 5 simple categories: Roads, Water, Garbage & Sanitation, Streetlights, and Parks.
  • Tested the app with local residents of all ages, trimming the reporting flow down to 3 quick steps: snap photo, confirm location, submit.
  • Grouped complaints submitted within 25 meters of each other so the ward office didn't get buried in duplicate tickets for the same pothole.
Project Outcomes

This replaced lost paper complaints and frantic phone calls with an open, trackable system. Residents can see that their issue was received and being worked on, and the councillor's office gets a clear queue of what needs fixing in the ward.
