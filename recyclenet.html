RecycleNet

Deep Learning-Assisted Dynamic Plastic Identification Technique For Automated Waste Segregation

RecycleNet is an edge computer vision system I built to identify plastic resin types in real time, covering all 6 standard Resin Identification Codes: PET, HDPE, PVC, LDPE, PP, and PS. I collected and labeled a custom dataset of 2,420 images into 8,884 bounding boxes, then benchmarked it against YOLO26n and RF-DETR — YOLO26n ran at 2.0 ms inference, RF-DETR hit 62.0% mAP@50. There's a multi-threaded Flask and OpenCV desktop pipeline for live inference, and a fully offline Android app in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose running PyTorch Lite. Published in IEEE ICCECE 2026.

Core Engineering Pillars

How the system sorts in real time on regular hardware and phones, with nothing running in the cloud.

1 Custom Dataset & 6 Resin Codes

Collected 2,420 images from multiple sources, annotated into 8,884 bounding boxes in Roboflow, across the 6 standard polymer codes: PET, HDPE, PVC, LDPE, PP, and PS.

2 2.0 ms Inference Loop (YOLO26n)

Benchmarked YOLO26n, YOLOv8s, YOLO26m, and RF-DETR on dual Nvidia T4 GPUs. YOLO26n came out fastest — 2.0 ms per frame, with 58.6% mAP@50.

3 Desktop OpenCV & Flask Pipeline

A multi-threaded Flask server paired with an async OpenCV video loop, running on either Apple Silicon (MPS) or Nvidia CUDA, falling back to CPU if none detected.

4 Offline Native Android (PyTorch Lite)

Built natively in Kotlin with Jetpack Compose and Material 3, using CameraX for live frame capture and PyTorch Lite models that run entirely on-device — no cloud connection needed.

The Challenge: Limitations of Legacy Industrial Methods

Plastic waste is piling up faster than recovery facilities can sort it, and sorting is the real bottleneck. The usual methods force a bad trade-off:

  • Manual Sorting: Inconsistent, error-prone, and exposes workers to chemical runoff and contaminants on the conveyor line.
  • Near-Infrared Spectrometry: Accurate, but each sensor costs tens of thousands of dollars, which limits it to large centralized plants.
  • What RecycleNet Does Instead: Uses a regular camera and an edge neural network to classify polymers and draw bounding boxes directly at the sorting line — no specialized sensor required.
Model Benchmarking: Accuracy vs. Inference Latency

To weigh speed against accuracy, I benchmarked four vision architectures on parallel Nvidia T4 GPUs:

  • YOLO26n, the Lightweight Option: 2.38M parameters, 2.0 ms inference, and 58.6% mAP@50 — fast enough for conveyor belts and mobile devices.
  • RF-DETR, the Most Accurate: 0.740 precision, 0.591 recall, and 62.0% mAP@50, at 14.8 ms per frame.
  • Compared to the Baseline: Against previously published YOLOv8 results, precision went from 57.4% to 72.3%, recall from 45.5% to 54.2%, and mAP@50 from 46.5% to 58.6%.
  • By Class: The confusion matrix showed strong separation for PET (81%) and LDPE (75%), and YOLO26n had fewer false positives on the background than the larger models.
Dual Deployment Architecture: Desktop Server & Mobile Client

To get this out of a notebook and into something usable on a real sorting line, I built two deployment paths:

  • Desktop, with OpenCV and Flask: Captures frames with cv2.VideoCapture, runs inference on Apple Silicon MPS or Nvidia CUDA, and streams the live result to an HTML5 dashboard.
  • Native Android App, in Kotlin: Built with Jetpack Compose and Material 3. Uses CameraX for continuous frame analysis and runs quantized PyTorch Lite (.ptl) models fully on-device — no cloud needed.
  • Two Modes on the App: Live Scan, which runs the camera feed in real time with confidence thresholding and NMS bounding boxes, and Gallery Mode, for analyzing a single static image.
Published Paper & Physical Sorting Future Scope

Published in the Proceedings of the 2026 IEEE International Conference on Computing, Electronics & Communications Engineering (ICCECE). Next step is connecting the detection output to Arduino and Raspberry Pi microcontrollers, to actually trigger pneumatic air nozzles and deflector arms — turning this into a working, low-cost sorting machine instead of just a detection model.
